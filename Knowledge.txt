Handstand
Type: Push
What it is: Holding your body upside down on your hands, in a straight line.
Beginner step: Wall handstand (chest facing the wall) for short holds, plus wrist warm-ups and shoulder strength.
Common mistake: Arching the lower back so the body looks like a banana.
Safety: Warm up your wrists first and learn how to fall out of the handstand safely.

Tuck Front Lever
Type: Pull
What it is: Hanging from a bar with straight arms, knees pulled to the chest, and the back parallel to the floor.
Beginner step: Active hang, scapular pull-ups, pull-ups with clean reps, hanging knee raises, and hollow body hold.
Common mistake: Bending the arms, letting the hips drop below the shoulders, or shrugging the shoulders up.
Safety: Warm up shoulders and elbows, increase hold time slowly, and stop if you feel sharp pain.

Front Lever
Type: Pull
What it is: Hanging from a bar with straight arms and a straight body, held level (parallel to the floor), facing up.
Beginner step: Tuck front lever first, then advanced tuck, then one-leg and straddle front lever.
Common mistake: Bending the arms, piking at the hips, or letting the hips drop below the shoulders.
Safety: Warm up shoulders and elbows, progress slowly, and avoid long holds when you are tired.

Tuck Planche
Type: Push
What it is: Holding your body off the ground on straight arms, knees tucked, with shoulders leaning forward past the hands.
Beginner step: Push-ups and dips, wrist conditioning, planche lean, frog stand, and pseudo planche push-ups.
Why lean forward: Leaning shifts your center of mass over your hands, so you stay balanced and can keep your body level instead of falling backward.
Common mistake: Bending the elbows, not leaning forward enough, or keeping the hips too high.
Safety: Warm up wrists and elbows well, because they take most of the load, and progress gradually.

Full Planche
Type: Push
What it is: Holding your whole body level, parallel to the floor, supported only by your hands, with straight arms and straight legs.
Beginner step: Tuck planche first, then advanced tuck planche, then straddle planche.
Common mistake: Bending the elbows, letting the hips sag or rise, and not leaning forward enough.
Safety: Build wrist, elbow and shoulder strength slowly over months or years, warm up well, and stop if you feel pain.
